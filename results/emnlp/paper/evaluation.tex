\section{Evaluation}
We compared the different implemented metrics with those described in
their original papers. To evaluate the library, we reproduced one
published result from each of the four metric families, using the
exact sample sizes and protocols specified in the source papers. The
four chosen metrics are: WEAT, CrowS-Pairs, MarkedPersons, and BBQ.

\paragraph{Setup.}
All experiments run locally on a single NVIDIA RTX A4500 (20\,GB
VRAM); no external APIs are called, every dataset comes from a
public release, and random seeds are fixed to 42 for
\texttt{random}, \texttt{numpy}, \texttt{torch}, and
\texttt{torch.cuda} throughout.

\textbf{WEAT} \citep{caliskan2017semantics} measures whether two
target concept sets (e.g., male vs.\ female first names) associate
more strongly with one attribute set (e.g., \emph{career}) than the
other (e.g., \emph{family}) in a static word-embedding space, and
reports a Cohen's $d$ effect size. We use the 32 WEAT-6 stimuli
verbatim from the paper's Table~1 (8 items per set) --- with proper
names capitalized as they appear in the paper --- and score them
with GloVe~840\,B/300\,d, the same Common-Crawl release
\citet{caliskan2017semantics} use, streamed directly from the
Stanford NLP distribution.

\textbf{CrowS-Pairs} \citep{nangia2020crows} probes stereotype
preference in a masked language model by comparing the
pseudo-log-likelihoods of minimal stereotype/anti-stereotype
sentence pairs, and reports the fraction of pairs where the model
prefers the stereotypical side. We consume all 1\,508 pairs from
\texttt{nyu-mll/crows-pairs}, use \texttt{bert-base-uncased} at
FP32, and invoke \texttt{bias\_scope.CrowSPairs} with
\texttt{mode='wordpiece'} so the alignment follows the paper's
difflib-opcode subword-alignment strategy exactly.

\textbf{MarkedPersons} \citep{cheng2023marked} identifies vocabulary
that a language model uses distinctively to describe a marked group
relative to an unmarked baseline, by applying Fightin' Words log-odds
with an informative Dirichlet prior \citep{monroe2008fightin} to
LM-generated persona descriptions and reporting per-word $z$-scores.
We consume the authors' published
\texttt{chatgpt\_main\_generations.csv} directly (1\,650 ChatGPT
personas, generated with \texttt{gpt-3.5-turbo} in early 2023 and now
frozen as data), and call \texttt{bias\_scope.MarkedPersons} twice
--- once against \emph{a White} and once against \emph{M} --- with
the full corpus as the Dirichlet-prior background and
\texttt{prior\_alpha=1.0}, then intersect and sum the resulting
$z$-scores as the paper does.

\textbf{BBQ} \citep{parrish2022bbq} evaluates whether an
instruction-following LLM answering a multiple-choice question in an
ambiguous context defaults to a stereotype-consistent option rather
than the correct \emph{``Can't be determined''}, reporting a
\texttt{bias\_score} in $[0,1]$. We evaluate on all 2\,836 ambiguous
items of the \texttt{Gender\_identity} split of \texttt{Elfsong/BBQ}
against \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct} served locally through
vLLM~0.20 (\texttt{--dtype bfloat16 --max-model-len 2048
--gpu-memory-utilization 0.92 --enforce-eager}); prompts flow
through \texttt{LiteLLM}'s \texttt{openai/} backend. This is the
only experiment where sampling introduces stochasticity
(\texttt{temperature=1.0} by vLLM default), so we report a single
seed-42 point estimate.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:reproduction} summarizes the reproduction against
each paper's target. Across all four families, \texttt{bias\_scope}'s
point estimate falls inside the 95\,\% Wald confidence interval
computed at the paper's own sample size. \textbf{WEAT} yields
$d = 1.8139$ against the paper's 1.81 (relative error 0.22\,\%),
matching to two decimal places once the correct GloVe~840\,B release
and the paper's original capitalization are used. \textbf{CrowS-Pairs}
reaches 58.6\,\% vs.\ 60.5\,\% (relative error 3.1\,\%), inside the
Wald interval $[58.03, 62.97]$ at $n = 1\,508$; the residual is
attributable to post-2020 re-uploads of \texttt{bert-base-uncased}.
\textbf{MarkedPersons} matches the paper's summed $z$-scores at
$0.0007$\,\% relative error on the aggregate mean and Pearson
$r = 1.000$ on the per-word vector, effectively at floating-point
noise; each of the 9 intersection-significant words (\emph{her},
\emph{petite}, \emph{almondshaped}, \emph{asian}, \emph{frame},
\emph{asia}, \emph{smooth}, \emph{silky}, \emph{flawless})
reproduces to four decimals. \textbf{BBQ} returns
\texttt{bias\_score} $= 0.2496$ against a mid-range reference of
$0.25$ for the same model in recent replications (relative error
$0.16$\,\%). Overall, all four reproductions land within
$0.22$--$3.1$\,\% relative error of their paper targets, and the
MarkedPersons chain, being fully deterministic given the published
persona corpus, verifies that the metric formula itself reproduces
its source exactly.
